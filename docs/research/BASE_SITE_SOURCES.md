# Base-site sources (Jezero crater, hypothetical crewed expedition)

Compiled 2026-11-15 for Ares Wayfinder. Scope: citable facts for choosing a base / landing-zone layout.
The expedition is HYPOTHETICAL; none of these sources endorses a crewed Jezero mission.

## How to read the "Verified" column

- **yes (PDF text)**: the source PDF was fetched and its text extracted locally; the figure was read in the extracted text.
- **yes (page)**: the source web page was opened and the statement read there.
- **yes (abstract/record)**: only the abstract or repository record was opened; full text not read.
- **snippet only**: seen only in a search-result summary; the source itself was NOT opened. Treat as unverified.
- **no**: could not be opened or confirmed. Do not cite the number.

Quotes are kept under 15 words. Everything else is paraphrase.

## What these sources do NOT support

- **No spatial radiation map of Jezero.** The only in-situ surface measurement cited is MSL RAD at Gale crater. No opened source reports a surface radiation measurement at Jezero, and none maps dose variation across the crater.
- **No explicit statement that Perseverance lacks a radiation detector.** It is inferred from NASA's instrument list. Word it that way.
- **No NASA-published numeric slope, rock-abundance or landing-area-size criterion for a human Mars landing zone** was confirmed. The only confirmed EZ numbers are +/-50 degrees latitude, +2 km MOLA elevation, ~100 km radius and 100 m landing accuracy. A "25 km2 landing site" figure was looked for and not found.
- **The 1 km plume keep-out is a concept-study planning value, not a requirement or a validated Mars plume result.** The source deck is internally inconsistent (700 m and 1000 m "diameter" circles versus a "1000 m radius" hazard zone). The ">1,000 m" and 2.0 km figures are lunar (vacuum, 1/6 g) and do not transfer to Mars directly.
- **Three Forks was not shown to be a certified lander site.** Sources say depot ground must be level and rock-free and that certification as a potential SRL site was "in progress" in 2022. No slope, rock-size or landing-circle number was confirmed.
- **Mars 2020 landing constraints are robotic** (sky crane, about 1 t rover). The 7.7 x 6.6 km ellipse and 600 m TRN divert say nothing about a crewed lander's needs.
- **MEDA figures are point measurements at the rover**, for the stated sol ranges (mostly northern spring and summer of MY 36). They are not a map of wind or dust-devil hazard across Jezero, and they do not cover a global dust storm.
- **No observed dust-devil areal density** (per km2 per sol) is given by Newman et al. 2022; the only areal number is from a simulation.
- **Hassler et al. 2014 was not read directly.** The 0.67 mSv/day value comes from the SwRI press release. Do not cite 0.64 mSv/day or 0.210 mGy/day from this file.
- **No insolation or day-length numbers for 18.4 N** were confirmed, only the Rucker et al. system-mass results for their assumed dust-storm case.
- **No crewed-rover or EVA slope limit for Mars** was found. LRV numbers are lunar design values; the Norcross grades are a test range.
